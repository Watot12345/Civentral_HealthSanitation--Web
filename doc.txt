                              CHAPTER 1

                           INTRODUCTION

      Local government units across the Philippines are under growing

pressure to deliver public services faster and with fewer errors, and

health and sanitation is one of the areas where this gap shows up the

most. In South Caloocan , the Health and Sanitation Office manages

everything from health center services and sanitation permits to

immunization records, wastewater requests, and disease surveillance,

but much of this still runs on scattered logbooks, spreadsheets, and

manual processes that slow down retrieval, monitoring, and reporting.

                                                                        11

       This study proposes a web-based Health and Sanitation

Management Information System that brings these services onto a

single platform and adds Gemini-powered AI to help staff spot trends

and generate reports faster, alongside a mobile app that lets residents

book appointments, apply for permits, and check records without

visiting an office in person.

1.1 Project Background and Motivation

       The continuous advancement of information technology has

significantly transformed how government agencies manage information

and deliver public services. One of the sectors that greatly benefits from

digital transformation is public health and sanitation, where timely,

accurate,   and    accessible   information is essential for protecting

community health and supporting efficient government operations.

       As urban populations continue to grow and public health demands

become increasingly complex, local government units (LGUs) are

encouraged to adopt integrated information systems that improve service

delivery, operational efficiency, and evidence-based decision-making.

                                                                        12

       In the Caloocan City Government, the Health and Sanitation Office

is responsible for managing various public health and sanitation services,

including health center services, sanitation permit processing and

inspections, immunization and nutrition monitoring, wastewater and

septic service requests, and health surveillance activities.

These services require the continuous collection, storage, and processing

of large volumes of information originating from different operational units.

When these records are maintained using fragmented or partially manual

processes, retrieving information, monitoring service delivery, generating

reports, and coordinating activities become more difficult, resulting in

delays and reduced operational efficiency.

       The increasing availability of Artificial Intelligence (AI) presents an

opportunity to improve traditional information systems by providing

intelligent data analysis and automation. AI technologies can process

large datasets, identify meaningful patterns, generate analytical insights,

and support evidence-based decision-making. In addition, AI can

automate     repetitive   tasks,   such   as   report   generation,    allowing

government personnel to focus on planning and implementing health and

sanitation   programs     rather   than   spending      significant   time    on

                                                                             13

administrative work. Recent advances in Generative Artificial Intelligence

have enabled government information systems to move beyond simple

record management by providing intelligent analytics, natural language

summarization, automated report generation, and decision

support capabilities.

       Unlike   conventional    management      information    systems that

primarily   store   and   retrieve   data,   AI-enhanced      systems    assist

administrators in interpreting information, identifying emerging trends,

and producing timely reports that support strategic planning and

operational     decision-making.      Within    the   proposed          system,

Geminipowered AI serves strictly as a decision-support tool by generating

summaries, identifying trends, and providing analytical insights, while

final decisions remain under the authority of authorized government

personnel. Citizens also experience challenges in accessing government

health services due to physical visits, manual applications, and limited

access to service information. Mobile technologies provide an opportunity

to improve public service accessibility by enabling citizens to request

services, submit applications, monitor requests, receive notifications, and

access selected records using their smartphones.

                                                                           14

      Recognizing these challenges, this study proposes the Design and

Development of a WebBased Health and Sanitation Management

Information System with Gemini-Powered AI Analytics, Decision Support,

and Automated Report Generation for the Caloocan City Government.

The proposed solution consists of a centralized web-based management

system for authorized personnel and a companion mobile application that

enables citizens to conveniently access selected health and sanitation

services such as appointment booking, permit application, immunization

record viewing, wastewater service requests, and health concern

reporting. By integrating Gemini-powered Artificial Intelligence (AI), the

system is expected to analyze collected information, generate analytical

insights, assist decisionmakers, and automate report preparation to

improve operational efficiency and support data-driven governance.

      The development of this system is expected to improve the

efficiency, accuracy, and reliability of health and sanitation information

management within the Caloocan City Government. It also supports the

ongoing digitalization efforts of local government units by introducing

innovative technologies that promote data-driven governance. Ultimately,

the proposed system seeks to strengthen public health and sanitation

                                                                      15

services, enhance operational efficiency, and contribute to better

decision-making that will benefit both government personnel and the

residents of Caloocan City.

1.2 Statement of the Problem

      The Health and Sanitation Office of the Caloocan City Government

plays a vital role in ensuring the implementation of health and sanitation

programs, monitoring compliance with health regulations, and processing

permits related to sanitation services. As the demand for these services

continues to increase, the office handles a growing volume of records and

documents that require efficient management and timely processing.

Despite the existing practices, several operational challenges continue to

affect the management of health and sanitation services. Patient

information, consultation records, and appointment schedules are

maintained across different records, making retrieval and monitoring

more time-consuming. Sanitation permit processing and inspections

require multiple manual verification procedures that may contribute to

processing delays. Immunization and nutrition records require continuous

monitoring   to   ensure timely vaccination and assessment, while

wastewater service requests often involve manual scheduling and status

                                                                      16

tracking. Likewise, disease surveillance activities require timely reporting

and consolidated information to support rapid public health response.

These operational processes generate significant amounts of information

that become difficult to manage efficiently when maintained through

fragmented or partially manual systems. Collectively, these operational

challenges demonstrate the need for an integrated and intelligent

information   system   capable    of   centralizing   records, streamlining

workflows, supporting analytical decision-making, improving citizen

access to government services, and enhancing operational efficiency.

Hence, this study proposes the Design and Development of a

Web-Based Health and Sanitation Management Information System with

Gemini-Powered AI Analytics, Decision Support, and Automated Report

Generation for the Caloocan City Government.

Specifically, this study seeks to answer the following questions:

   ● How can a web-based Health and Sanitation Management

       Information System centralize the management of health and

       sanitation information for the Caloocan City Government?

   ● How can the proposed system improve the efficiency of Health

       Center Services, Sanitation Permit Management, Immunization

                                                                        17

      and Nutrition Management, Wastewater Services, and Health

      Surveillance through digitalization?

   ● How can Gemini-powered AI analytics support data analysis,

      decision support, and operational planning? • How can automated

      report generation improve report preparation?

   ● How can the citizen mobile application improve accessibility to

      government health and sanitation services? How acceptable is the

      proposed system in terms of the following ISO/IEC 25010 quality

      characteristics:

   ● Functional Suitability;

   ● Performance Efficiency;

   ● Compatibility; • Usability;

   ● Reliability; • Security;

   ● Maintainability;

   ● Portability.

1.6 Definition of Terms

       The following terms are defined to provide a clearer understanding

of the key concepts used in this study. Artificial Intelligence (AI). Artificial

Intelligence refers to the ability of computer systems to perform tasks that

normally require human intelligence, such as analyzing data, recognizing

patterns, and generating insights. In this study, it refers to the

Gemini-powered AI integrated into the proposed system to analyze health

and sanitation data, provide decision support, and assist in automated

report generation. Automated Report Generation.

       Automated report generation is the process of creating reports

using computer systems with minimal manual intervention. In this study, it

                                                                            29

refers to the system feature that automatically prepares health and

sanitation reports based on the data stored in the database. Centralized

Database. A centralized database is a single repository where data is

stored and managed in one location.

       In this study, it refers to the database used by the proposed

system to securely store and organize health and sanitation records for

authorized users. Citizen Mobile Application. A mobile-based application

developed as part of the proposed system that enables citizens to access

selected health and sanitation services such as appointment booking,

permit application, immunization record viewing, wastewater service

requests, request tracking, and health concern reporting. Dashboard. A

graphical interface that presents key performance indicators, system

summaries, alerts, notifications, and operational statistics to support

monitoring and decision-making.

       Dashboard Analytics. Dashboard analytics refers to the visual

presentation of processed data, trends, and performance indicators on

the system dashboard. In this study, it refers to the charts, sparklines, and

summary indicators that allow personnel to monitor health center, permit,

                                                                        30

immunization, wastewater, and surveillance activity at a glance. Decision

Support.

       The use of analytical tools and AI-generated insights to assist

authorized personnel in evaluating information, identifying trends, and

making informed decisions. In this study, Gemini-powered AI provides

summaries, recommendations, and analytical outputs that support, but do

not replace, human decision-making. Electronic Medical Record (EMR).

An Electronic Medical Record is a digital version of a patient's health

information, including consultations, diagnoses, prescriptions, and

treatment history. In this study, it refers to the patient records maintained

under the Health Center Services module. Gemini-Powered AI Analytics.

Gemini-powered AI analytics refers to the application of Google's Gemini

AI in examining data to identify patterns, trends, and useful information.

In this study, it refers to the integration of Gemini AI in the proposed

system to analyze health and sanitation data and generate meaningful

insights for decision-making.

       Health and Sanitation Management Information System. A Health

and Sanitation Management Information System is a computerized

system designed to collect, manage, process, and store health and

                                                                        31

sanitation information. In this study, it refers to the proposed web-based

system developed for the Caloocan City Government to manage health

records, sanitation permits, immunization, wastewater services, health

surveillance, and related reports. Health Center Services. Health Center

Services refer to the range of activities involved in delivering primary

healthcare, including patient registration, consultations, appointments,

prescriptions, referrals, and triage. In this study, it refers to the module

that manages these activities within the proposed system. Health

Surveillance.

       Health surveillance is the continuous collection, analysis, and

interpretation of health-related data for monitoring public health

conditions. In this study, it refers to the module of the proposed system

that records health cases, monitors disease trends, and supports

outbreak detection and response. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) are measurable values used to

monitor and evaluate the performance of an organization, program, or

process. In this study, KPIs refer to the performance metrics displayed on

the system dashboard to help authorized personnel monitor health and

sanitation activities and support decision-making.

                                                                        32

       Management Information System (MIS). A computer-based

information system that collects, processes, stores, and presents

information    to    support    organizational operations and managerial

decision-making. In this study, it refers to the proposed Health and

Sanitation Management Information System for the Caloocan City

Government. Mobile Application.

       A mobile application is software designed to run on smartphones

and other mobile devices. In this study, it refers to the citizen application

that enables users to book appointments, apply for sanitation permits,

request services, view immunization records, track requests, and report

health and sanitation concerns. Notification System. A notification system

is a feature that delivers timely alerts and updates to users through

channels such as email, SMS, push notifications, or in-app messages.

       In this study, it refers to the mechanism that informs citizens and

personnel about appointments, permit status, immunization schedules,

wastewater service requests, and public health advisories. Role-Based

Access Control (RBAC). Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is a security

approach      that   limits    system   access   based   on   the roles and

responsibilities assigned to each user. In this study, it refers to the system

                                                                         33

feature that allows authorized personnel to access only the modules and

functions that are relevant to their assigned roles, helping protect

2.3 Agile Scrum Methodology

Agile Methodology

      Agile Methodology is a software development approach that

focuses on flexibility, collaboration, and continuous improvement

throughout the development process. Instead of completing the entire

                                                                    43

system before testing and evaluation, Agile divides the project into

smaller and manageable phases that allow developers to continuously

build, test, and improve the system. This approach enables development

teams to quickly respond to changing requirements and stakeholder

feedback while maintaining the quality of the software being developed.

       One of the main principles of Agile is the continuous delivery of

functional software. Rather than waiting until the end of the project, each

completed    feature   is   evaluated and improved before the next

development cycle begins. This iterative process minimizes development

risks, improves communication among team members, and ensures that

the final product satisfies the needs of its intended users.

Scrum Framework

       Scrum is one of the most widely used frameworks under the Agile

Methodology. It provides a structured process for managing software

development through clearly defined roles, events, and artifacts. Scrum

allows development teams to organize their work into short development

cycles known as sprints. Each sprint focuses on completing a set of

                                                                       44

prioritized system functionalities that can be tested and evaluated before

moving to the next sprint.

       The Scrum Team consists of three primary roles. The Product

Owner identifies and prioritizes the system requirements based on

stakeholder needs. The Scrum Master ensures that Scrum practices are

properly   implemented       and   helps the team overcome obstacles

encountered    during    development.    The   Development     Team     is

responsible for designing, developing, testing, and delivering working

software at the end of every sprint.

       The Scrum framework also follows several important events that

guide the development process. Sprint Planning is conducted before

each sprint to determine the tasks and objectives to be accomplished.

During the sprint, the team continuously monitors its progress and

collaborates to resolve development issues. At the end of every sprint, a

Sprint Review is conducted to demonstrate the completed functionalities

to stakeholders and gather feedback. Finally, the Sprint Retrospective

allows the team to evaluate its performance and identify improvements

for the succeeding sprint.

                                                                      45

       In addition, Scrum utilizes several important artifacts. The Product

Backlog contains the complete list of system requirements and desired

functionalities. The Sprint Backlog consists of the selected tasks that will

be completed during the current sprint. After the sprint is completed, the

finished functionalities become the Product Increment, representing a

working version of the system that is ready for testing and evaluation.

       The researchers selected the Agile Scrum Methodology for the

2.4.1 Microservices Architecture

       Microservices Architecture is a software architectural approach

that structures an application into a collection of small, independent, and

loosely coupled services. Each service is designed to perform a specific

business function and can be developed, deployed, maintained, and

scaled independently without affecting the other services within the

application. Communication between these services is commonly

                                                                       50

achieved through Application Programming Interfaces (APIs), allowing

them to exchange data and perform coordinated operations efficiently.

According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST),

microservices architecture has become a preferred approach for

developing enterprise and cloud-based applications because of its agility,

scalability, modularity, and support for independent deployment of

application components.

      Compared with traditional monolithic architecture, Microservices

Architecture enables development teams to divide a complex system into

smaller and more manageable services. This approach simplifies

software maintenance, accelerates system updates, and minimizes the

impact of failures since modifications made to one service do not

necessarily affect the entire application. It also supports continuous

integration and deployment, allowing organizations to introduce new

features and improvements more efficiently while maintaining system

availability and performance.

3.2 Architectural Design Decisions

       The proposed system uses Microservices Architecture as the

main architectural approach for the integrated LGU system. The system

is divided into different services, where each service is responsible for a

specific government function. The Health and Sanitation Management

System is one of these services and focuses on health and

sanitation-related activities.

       This architecture was chosen because the different services

have different functions and requirements. Separating them makes the

system easier to develop, update, and maintain. It also allows the

different services to communicate with each other when needed while

keeping their own functions separate.

3.2.1 Why Microservices?

              Microservices was chosen because the whole LGU

       system contains many different services, such as health and

       sanitation, permits and licensing, social services, education,

       disaster response, and revenue services. Each service handles a

                                                                        93

different type of government activity, so placing all of them into

one large application may make the system harder to manage.

      With microservices, each service can focus on its own

tasks. For the Health and Sanitation Management System, it

handles Health Center Services, Sanitation Permit and

Inspection, Immunization and Nutrition Tracker, Wastewater

and Septic Services, and Health Surveillance System. This

allows the team to work on the health and sanitation functions

without making major changes to the other services.

      This approach also makes it easier to improve the system

in the future. If one service needs an update or additional

features, the changes can be made mainly within that service.

This helps reduce the effect of changes on the other parts of the

integrated LGU system.

3.2.2 Service Independence

      Each service in the LGU system has its own specific

responsibilities. The Health and Sanitation Management System

focuses only on health and sanitation-related services.

                                                               94

This separation allows the team to develop and maintain the

health and sanitation service without directly changing the

internal functions of other LGU services. It also makes it easier to

find and fix problems within a specific service.

3.2.3 API-Based Communication

       The different services communicate with each other

through Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). APIs

allow one service to request or send information to another

service when needed.

For example, the Health and Sanitation service may need

information from another LGU service for a specific process.

Instead of directly accessing the other service's internal

functions, the services can communicate through their available

APIs. This helps keep the services separate while still allowing

them to work together.

3.2.4 Scalability

                                                                 95

      Scalability was considered because the number of users

and records may increase as the system is used by more people.

With microservices, a service that receives more users or

requests can be given additional resources without having to

increase the resources of the entire LGU system.

      For the Health and Sanitation service, this can be useful

when the number of health records, permit requests, service

requests, or health surveillance records increases over time.

3.2.5 Maintainability

      Microservices also makes the system easier to maintain

because the different services are separated based on their

functions. Developers can work on a specific service when fixing

errors, updating features, or making improvements.

      For the Health and Sanitation service, changes can focus

on its own functions such as health center records, sanitation

permits, immunization records, wastewater requests, and health

surveillance without requiring major changes to unrelated

services.

3.2.6 Security and Access Control

      Security is also considered because the system handles

health and other important government information. Only

authorized users should be able to access information and

functions based on their assigned roles.

      The system uses authentication and access control to

help limit access to authorized users. Communication between

services should also be protected to help prevent unauthorized

access to information. These security considerations are

important because the proposed system manages records that

should only be available to the appropriate users.

the information available in the system.

      The system uses analytics to help users understand the

3.4.3 Decision Support

       The system provides decision support by presenting

useful information based on the records available in the system.

The analytics and AI-generated insights can help authorized

personnel understand current conditions, compare information,

and identify possible concerns.

       The decision-support feature is intended only as an aid for

users. Final decisions remain with the authorized personnel who

are responsible for evaluating the information and deciding what

action should be taken.

       The system includes automated report generation to help

reduce the time needed to prepare reports manually. The system

3.4.5 Health and Sanitation Monitoring

      The system supports monitoring of different health and

sanitation activities. For the Health Surveillance System,

recorded health-related information can be reviewed to help

identify possible changes or patterns in reported cases.

      For Sanitation Permit and Inspection, the system allows

authorized personnel to monitor permit records, inspection

schedules, inspection results, and related activities. These

features provide a more organized way of tracking health and

sanitation processes compared to relying only on manual

records.

      The system uses data visualization to present information

through dashboards, charts, graphs, and other visual elements.

used for activities such as inspection schedules, permit-related

tasks, immunization reminders, and other events supported by

the system.

monitoring, reporting, and decision-making. This provides a more useful

and organized approach to managing health and sanitation services

within the integrated LGU system.
